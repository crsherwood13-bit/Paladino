# Investment Banking Website Design Reference

Synthesized from 8 boutique and bulge-bracket advisory firm homepages (Evercore, Lazard,
Moelis, Centerview Partners, PJT Partners, Rothschild & Co, Jefferies, William Blair) as
design reference for the Paladino site. Sourced 2026-07-08.

## Common patterns across firms

**Color**
- Overwhelmingly white/off-white backgrounds with near-black or charcoal body text.
- One dominant brand accent, used sparingly: navy or corporate blue (Jefferies, Moelis,
  Centerview), or full black/white high-contrast (PJT). Gold/orange shows up only as a
  minor accent (Evercore's orange in transaction logos), never as a dominant field color.
- No gradients, no saturated multi-color palettes. Restraint signals credibility.

**Typography**
- Sans-serif across the board for UI and body copy (no firm in the sample runs serif body
  text, despite the "old money" reputation of the sector).
- Hierarchy built from weight and size, not color: bold, larger headlines; regular-weight,
  smaller body text. Generous line height.
- Headlines lean short and declarative ("A Global Firm Built Over Generations," "Client-
  Focused, Values-Driven") rather than clever or casual.

**Layout**
- Sticky/horizontal top nav with dropdown mega-menus grouped by capability (Advisory,
  Restructuring, Wealth Management, etc.).
- Hero: full-width image or video, one short headline, minimal or no CTA button clutter.
- Below the fold: modular card grids — key stats (deal value, headcount, years founded),
  featured transactions with client logos, news/press cards, office locations.
- Heavy use of whitespace and vertical rhythm; sections are clearly delineated, not
  seamlessly blended.
- Footer is dense and functional: multi-column links, office directory, legal/compliance
  text, disclaimers.

**Imagery**
- Either (a) minimal imagery, text- and logo-led (Lazard, Centerview), or (b) large
  professional photography of offices, skylines, and people in business settings (Moelis,
  PJT, Rothschild, Jefferies, William Blair). No illustration, no stock-photo cliché
  handshakes — photography is composed, high-production, slightly desaturated.
- Client/transaction logos are used as social proof and credibility signaling, arranged in
  clean grids.

**Tone / messaging**
- Scale and longevity metrics ("$4 trillion," "175+ years," "50 countries") feature
  prominently near the top of the page.
- Copy favors words like "client-focused," "trusted," "independent," "discretion" over
  growth-hacky or casual language.

## Recommendations for Paladino

- Base palette: off-white/white background, near-black text, one navy or deep charcoal
  accent for nav/CTAs. Reserve a secondary accent (e.g., a muted gold or blue) for
  highlights only — never as a large color field.
- Sans-serif type system throughout; establish hierarchy via weight/size, not color.
- Hero: single strong photograph or abstract architectural/skyline image + one-line
  positioning statement, no carousel needed at launch.
- Below the fold: 3-stat credibility band (even if placeholder figures at launch), service
  cards, and a transactions/case-study grid once available.
- Keep the footer dense and information-rich — it's a trust signal in this sector, not
  just navigation.
- Avoid anything that reads as consumer/SaaS-marketing (bright multi-color gradients,
  playful illustration, casual copy). Restraint is the differentiator.
