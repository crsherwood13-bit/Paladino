# Commercial GC Price Ladder: who buys at each price, and how hard it is to get there

Goal: **$25k/month profit.** Channel: cold email, with calls used sparingly.
Offer: the Commercial Bid Pipeline (meetings with property owners, developers and facility managers).

> **Estimate vs. fact.** The market count (about 39,000) comes from Census data, and the margin ranges come from industry benchmarks. The split by company size, the reply rates and the close rates are working estimates. Replace them with your real numbers after your first 1,000 sends and your first 10 calls.

---

## 1. Market size (TAM / SAM)

**TAM:** about **39,000** US commercial and institutional building contractor establishments (NAICS 236220, Census County Business Patterns 2023). They have about 640,000 employees, roughly 16 per establishment on average.

**Estimated split by size** (construction skews small):

| Staff | Est. firms | Typical revenue | Who signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | ~24,000 | under $5M | Owner. Too small for this offer. |
| 10–49 | ~11,000 | $5M–$40M | Owner / President |
| 50–99 | ~2,200 | $30M–$100M | Owner + CFO, often a BD director |
| 100+ | ~1,800 | $75M–$1B+ | VP BD / Marketing, CFO, sometimes procurement |

(GC revenue per employee is high because subcontractors do most of the field work.)

**SAM: the firms you can actually reach and sell to**
- Remove firms that are mostly residential but miscoded, those with no findable owner email, and those with no website or no recent activity. Expect about **60%** of each band to be reachable.
- **10–49 staff:** about **6,500** reachable firms
- **50–99 staff:** about **1,300**
- **100+ staff:** about **1,100**, but most already have marketing or BD staff

**The constraint you'll actually hit is list exhaustion.** Your whole realistic market is about 9,000 firms. Selling $7.5k deals that each need about 600 prospects, you burn through the 10–49 band in under a year. That limit forces you up the price ladder.

**Margin context:** commercial GCs typically run **15–25% gross margin and 5–10% net**. On a $1M job they clear roughly $150k–$250k gross profit. That figure is the gap you anchor the price against.

## 2. The ladder

### $7.5k: first clients (no proof yet)

- **Who buys:** 10–49 staff, $5M–$25M revenue. The owner still does business development personally.
- **Who signs:** the owner, alone. They can pay by card or check without asking anyone.
- **Gap math:** one negotiated job is worth roughly $75k–$250k in gross profit, so $7.5k is about 3–10% of one job. Easy to justify.
- **Proof needed:** none, apart from the guarantee and your 5 prepared names. The 218-replies proof helps a little.
- **Sales cycle:** 1–3 weeks. Discovery, scope, close.
- **Resistance you'll hear:**
  - "Who are you? You're not in construction." You need to sound like a peer. Use their words: backlog, negotiated, owners.
  - "We tried marketing and it didn't work." Most of them have been burned by an SEO or website agency.
  - "We only do relationship work."
  - Cash flow is lumpy. If they're waiting on a draw, they'll stall.
- **Attainability:** **high.** This is where your first 2–3 clients come from.
- **Funnel per close (estimate):** about 600 prospects → about 9 positive replies (1.5%) → about 6 calls held → about 1.5 scopes → 1 close.

### $15k: after 1–2 case studies

- **Who buys:** the top of the 10–49 band ($20M–$40M) and the 50–99 band.
- **Who signs:** the owner, often with a partner or CFO looking over it.
- **Gap math:** still under 10% of one good job.
- **Proof needed:** at least **one real result** ("[GC] got 9 owner meetings in 45 days, 2 turned into bids"), plus a reference they can call.
- **Sales cycle:** 3–6 weeks, often with a second decision maker added to call 2.
- **Resistance:**
  - "Can we start smaller?" Hold the price and offer to cut scope instead.
  - The CFO wants ROI. The scope's gap section handles that.
  - "Let me see results from the first month first." Offer 50/50 payment terms.
- **Attainability:** **medium–high** once you have the case study. Without one, it's hard.
- **Funnel per close:** roughly the same reply rates. Close rates drop a little, but each deal is worth twice as much.

### $25k: termsheetinator's floor ($25k–$50k)

- **Who buys:** 50–99 staff, $30M–$100M, usually with a named BD director or VP of Preconstruction.
- **Who signs:** owner plus CFO, sometimes the president's operating group.
- **Gap math:** jobs at this size are $2M–$10M, so one job means $300k–$1.5M in gross profit. The $25k fee is small next to that, but they need to believe you can deliver.
- **Proof needed:** **2–3 case studies** with real numbers, references, and a proper agreement. A 1-page scope may not be enough. They'll want to see a process.
- **Sales cycle:** 4–10 weeks. Expect 3 calls and possibly a meeting with their BD lead.
- **Resistance:**
  - "We have a BD person for this." Position yourself as more meetings for that person, not a replacement.
  - "Why not just hire someone?" A BD hire costs $100k–$150k a year, takes 6 months to ramp and may not work out.
  - Concern about their brand being emailed. Show them the copy and the volume limits.
  - Comparing you with real agencies.
- **Attainability:** **medium**, realistically from month 4–6 on.
- **This is where $25k/month gets easy:** 1 deal a month plus a few retainers.

### $50k+: later, if at all

- **Who buys:** 100+ staff and $100M+ revenue. They already have marketing and BD teams.
- **What they'd buy:** a full program across several offices or markets, data, and training their BD team. That's more than meetings.
- **Proof needed:** strong case studies at their size, plus referrals.
- **Sales cycle:** 2–4 months, possibly with procurement and an RFP.
- **Attainability:** **low for now.** Don't target this tier in your first year.

## 3. The realistic path to $25k/month profit

| Months | Price | Target | Closes/month | Revenue/month | Hard costs | Profit/month |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | $7.5k | 10–49 staff | 1–2 | $7.5k–$15k | ~$1k per client | ~$6.5k–$13k |
| 3–6 | $15k | top of 10–49, 50–99 | 1–2 | $15k–$30k | ~$1k per client | ~$14k–$28k |
| 6+ | $25k | 50–99 | 1 | $25k | ~$1.5k per client | ~$23k |
| + retainers | $2.5k/month each | past clients | stacks up | +$2.5k each | ~$300 each | +$2.2k each |

**Honest read:**
- At $7.5k you need **4 closes a month** to hit $25k profit. That's unlikely without proof, and it burns your list fast.
- **Expect to hit $25k/month profit around months 4–6.** That assumes you land 2–3 pilots early, deliver real results, raise the price to $15k, and turn 3–4 past clients into retainers.
- After that, **1 deal at $25k plus 4 retainers** gets you about $33k/month in profit.

## 4. Logistics and operations

**Sending (for selling to GCs):**
- About 600–1,200 new prospects a month means roughly 3,000 emails a month including follow-ups.
- At about 30 emails per inbox per day, that's **6–8 inboxes on 3–4 domains**.
- Use Google Workspace or Outlook, never Gmail accounts.
- Cost is about $150–250/month.

**Delivery (for each client):**
- Each client gets 6–10 inboxes on 3 of **their own** domains (similar to their brand), plus a data tool and a sending tool.
- Hard costs are about $300–500 to build, plus about $200/month to run. That's where the ~$1k per-pilot figure comes from.
- Your time is 15–20 hours to build, then 3–5 hours a week to run and to handle replies.

**Capacity:**
- Solo, you can run about **6–8 active clients** before reply handling eats your week.
- Past that, hire a VA for reply management at about $800–1,200/month.

**The client's own market is finite:**
- A metro might have only 1,500–4,000 property owners, developers and facility managers.
- Promising 8 meetings in 45 days is realistic in a mid-size metro and tight in a small town.
- **Disqualify GCs in towns under about 150k population**, or lower the guarantee for them.

**Exclusivity:**
- Take only one GC per metro and specialty. That's real scarcity, and it protects your results.
- It also means your best metros fill up, which pushes you toward new metros or adjacent niches.

**Timing:**
- Owners are on jobsites, so the best call windows are **6:30–7:30am and 4–5:30pm** local time.
- Q4 and Q1 are planning season for next year's work, which is a good time to sell.
- Summer is busy, with slower replies but real pain if backlog is thin.

**Guarantee risk:**
- "Keep working until 8 meetings" can eat your margin on a weak market.
- Cap it in the agreement: "up to 60 additional days."

## 5. Where else the same offer works (once GCs are proven)

Same gap, same mechanism, different market. Use these when GC metros fill up or the list runs out:
- Commercial roofing contractors (they sell to property managers and facility directors)
- Commercial mechanical / HVAC contractors (same buyers)
- Commercial electrical contractors
- Design-build and tenant-improvement specialists
- Commercial cleaning and facility services (lower ticket, much bigger market)
