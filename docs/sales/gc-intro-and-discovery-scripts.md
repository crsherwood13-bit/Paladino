# Commercial GC: Intro Call + Discovery Call Scripts

Built from two of Charles's existing scripts:
- **Intro call** (after a positive reply): adapted from *7FO Intro Call Sales Script (2-Call Close)*.
- **Discovery call**: adapted from the *Vantage & Verity Discovery-to-Close* script (NEPQ questioning + Leo Moore boxing), with an optional **Call 2 close** when they can't decide on call 1.

**The flow:**
```
Positive reply → INTRO CALL (10–15 min) → DISCOVERY CALL (30–40 min)
                                              ├─ Boxing all "yes" + decision maker on the call → CLOSE ON THE CALL
                                              └─ Otherwise → send 1-page scope → CALL 2: CLOSE (15–20 min)
```

**Rules for every call:**
- Never say "AI," "outbound," "automation," "sequences," "leads" or "funnel." Say: *meetings with property owners, negotiated work, getting in before the bid, steady backlog.*
- Never say a price before the prospect has told you what the problem costs them.
- One question at a time. Ask it, then stop talking.
- If the intro call is going well and they have time, **skip booking a second call and go straight into the Discovery Call.**

---

# PART 1: INTRO CALL (positive reply → book discovery)

**Goal:** find out why they replied, what they do now, and whether there's a real problem. Then book the Discovery Call. Don't pitch.

## Opening

**Outbound: you're calling them off a positive reply**
> "Hey [Name], it's Charles. It looks like you replied to my email the other day about getting in front of property owners before projects go out to bid. I just had time to get back to you. Do you maybe have 5 to 10 minutes now, or later this week, so I can ask a few questions and see if we could even help?"

- **"I have time now"** → go to **Connecting Q1**.
- **Not now** → book a time and call them then: *"No problem. I'm looking at my calendar. Does [day] at 7am or 4:30pm work better? I know you're probably on a jobsite during the day."*

**Inbound: they booked a call with you**
> "So it looks like you booked some time with me about possibly getting outside help winning more commercial work. This first call is really for me to learn what you've done in the past to bring in new projects, what you're doing now, and what you're looking for, so I can see if we can even help. Do you mind sharing what you're looking to accomplish?"

Then go to **Connecting Q1**.

---

## Connecting phase
**Goal: find the real reason they're on the call. It sets up everything after it.**

**Q1:** "Can I ask, out of curiosity, what caught your attention when you saw my email in the first place?"

**Q2:** "Okay, and was there anything else that caught your attention?"

**Q3:** "And just so I understand, do you know specifically what you're looking for when it comes to winning new work? More projects, bigger projects, a certain type?"

---

## Situation phase
**Goal: find out what they're doing now to win work.**

**Q1:** "What are the different ways you bring in new projects right now?"
*(Expect: referrals, repeat clients, bid boards, Dodge, the same few developers.)*

**Q2:** "And that approach, [referrals / bid boards], how long have you been doing it that way?"

**Q3:** "Just out of curiosity, roughly how much of your work is negotiated versus stuff you're hard-bidding against other GCs?"

**Q3 follow-up:** "Okay, so about [X]% negotiated. That's pretty common." *(Write the number down.)*

**Q4:** "And how many months of backlog are you sitting on right now?"

---

## Problem awareness phase
**Goal: get to their problem and how it affects the business.**

**Q1:** "Do you like your current way of bringing in new work?"

- **If no:** "What don't you like about it?" → "If anything, what else don't you like about it?"
- **If yes:** "Right, and what do you like about it?" → "What else?" → "It sounds like things are going fairly well. Is there anything you'd change, if you could, about the *type* of projects you're getting, or the *number* of them?"

**Once they name a problem:**

**Clarify:** "What do you mean when you say [their words]?"

**Q2:** "How long has that been going on?"

**Q3:** "And what do you think caused it?"

**Q4:** "This problem of [their words], has it had an impact on growing the company?"
→ "How so?"

**Q5:** "[Name], just so I understand why you're on this call with me: I get that you want more work, but what's the main reason you're looking at outside help instead of just [bidding more / leaning on referrals / hiring a BD person]?"

---

## Transition to booking the Discovery Call

Give the 30,000-foot overview, **then ask for the call immediately**:

> "Great. [Name], I appreciate you letting me learn a bit about the company. Just so you have an idea of what we do without boring you to death: about 20% of it is finding the property owners, developers and facility managers in your area who have projects coming up. The other 80% is the back end. We make sure the conversations that come in are real projects that fit what you build, and we get them on your calendar before they go out to bid, so you're not one of seven GCs on the bid board."

> "If you're open to it, I'd love 30 minutes later this week or early next week to dig into what you're looking to accomplish. *(pause)* I'm looking at my calendar now. I have time later this week and next week. What day works best for you?"

**They say "I'll get back to you on a time":**
> "Not a problem, [Name]. Do you have a rough timeframe for getting back to me? I'm hard to catch randomly, and I want to make sure I'm free for you."

Then:
> "And if you'd like, I can pull up my calendar and you pull up yours, and we pick a time now. That way you're not chasing me down and vice versa. Would that work?"

---

## When to push for the Discovery Call (and when not to)

1. **Buying language:** "I'd be interested in *seeing* how you do it." "How does that work?" → book it.
2. **How they reacted to the overview:**
   - Curious, listening, asking questions → push for the call.
   - Defensive ("We've tried that, don't waste my time") → go to the "what makes you different" answers below. If they're still cold, don't push. Offer to send the 3 owner names instead.
3. **Listen to what they actually say.** This is the key one.
4. **"We've tried marketing before, what makes you different?"**
   - **Real projects only.** "We only count an owner if they have an actual project that fits what you build, and you approve the criteria up front."
   - **Before the bid.** "Dodge and the bid boards show you what every GC sees. We get you the conversation before there's a bid."
   - **You own it.** "It's built under your company's name, and you keep the whole system at the end."

**The one-liner that builds trust instantly** (negative → positive → neutral):
> "We learned we can't just send you a pile of names *(negative)*. What we do is make sure every owner we put in front of you has a real project that fits what you build *(positive)*, so your time goes to work you'd actually want to win *(neutral)*."

# PART 2: DISCOVERY CALL (Leo Moore boxing)

**Follow the sequence. Skipping steps loses you control of the call.**

Have the **Numbers Sheet** open and fill it in as you go:
```
Goal (revenue/backlog, by when): ______   Today: ______   Backlog (months): ______
% negotiated vs hard bid: ____ / ____     Hard-bid win rate: ____   Negotiated win rate: ____
GP on a typical job: $______              Jobs needed to hit goal: ______
GAP ($) = jobs needed × GP per job: $______  ← THEY say it out loud
Who decides / who else signs off: ______
```

## 00: Frame (say it word for word)
> "Appreciate you taking the time. I'll keep this straightforward. I want to understand how you're winning work right now, where that's working and where it's not, and then if it makes sense, I'll walk you through how we get GCs in front of owners before projects go out to bid. If it's not relevant, no worries at all."

## 01: Discovery
**Get them talking. Listen, don't pitch. One question, then let them answer.**

- **Situation:** "Walk me through how you're winning work right now."
- **Split:** "What's that split look like, hard bid versus negotiated or repeat clients?"
- **Performance:** "How's that been working from a margin standpoint?"
- **Overlap:** "On the hard bids, how many other GCs are you usually up against?"
- **Goal:** "Where do you want the business to be by the end of next year? Revenue, backlog, whatever number you watch." → "So the target is [X]. Is that right?"

## 02: Pain
**Don't rush this. Pain is why they buy. Let it breathe, one question at a time.**

- "What's been the most frustrating part of that so far?"
- "How long has that been an issue?"
- "What has it actually affected? Margins, keeping crews busy, the backlog, being able to plan?"

**Put a number on it (fill in the Numbers Sheet):**
- "Where are you today against the target?"
- "On a typical job, what do you clear in gross profit? Ballpark is fine."
- "So roughly how many more jobs would it take to hit [goal]?"
- "So that's about [jobs × GP] in gross profit you need to find that isn't on the books yet. Is that fair?"

**Stop talking until they confirm it. Circle the number.**

## 03: Failed attempts
**Show the problem isn't new and they haven't solved it. This heads off "we'll handle it internally" before they say it.**

- "Have you tried building anything to go after owners directly? A BD hire, marketing, networking events?"
- "What happened with that?"
- "Anything else you've tried?"

## 04: Boxing (the Leo Moore framework, don't skip any)
**You're building the logical case for moving forward. Get a clear answer to each one.**

1. **Internal solve:** "Is there a reason you haven't just built this internally already?"
2. **Belief:** "Does having a steady way to get in front of owners before the bid actually make sense for you guys?"
3. **Positive future:** "If you had a steady stream of conversations with owners who have real projects coming up, how would that change things?"
4. **Negative future:** "If nothing changes, what do the next 6 to 12 months look like?" *(Write their answer word for word.)*
5. **Timing:** "What's made this more of a priority now?"

**Who decides** (add this before the transition):
> "If this made sense, is that a decision you'd make on your own, or would a partner or your CFO want to look at it too?"

## 05: Transition (one line, don't elaborate)
> "Got it. What you're describing is exactly what we see with most commercial GCs your size."

## 06: Pitch (one idea, keep it tight)
> "We build you a channel that reaches property owners, developers and facility managers in your area, so you're not relying on the bid board. You're having conversations with owners before those projects ever go out to bid."

Then pause and let them react. If they ask how, give 2 to 3 sentences, max:
> "We find the owners with projects coming up: new permits, property sales, companies expanding. We reach out under your company's name, and only the ones with a real project that fits what you build land on your calendar. You approve exactly what counts as a real opportunity before we start."

---

## DECISION POINT: close now, or take it to Call 2?

**Close on this call (go to 07 and 08) only if ALL of these are true:**
- [ ] They said yes to the Belief and Positive Future questions with no hesitation.
- [ ] They said the gap number out loud.
- [ ] They named a real reason for the timing.
- [ ] The person who signs is on the call.

**Otherwise, use the 2-call close:**
> "Based on what you told me, there's a real gap here, about [gap $]. Rather than throw a number at you now, let me put together a one-page plan built around [City], including the owners I'd go after first, and the exact criteria for what counts as a real opportunity, so you can approve it. Can we do 20 minutes on [day] at [time] to go through it? *(If someone else decides:)* And it'd make sense to have [partner/CFO] on that one too."

> "One ask before then: when I send it, give me your first reaction. What fits, what's off, what's missing."

Send the 1-page scope within 24 hours (template in `gc-sales-playbook.md`, Stage 5).

---

## 07: Pricing (on this call or Call 2)
**Lead with the 90-day mandate. Anchor on the outcome and the gap, not the fee.**

> "We start with a 90-day mandate to build and run the system for you. The goal is [X] real opportunities, owners with projects that meet the criteria you approve. The investment is [PRICE], half to start and half at day 45. Compared to the [gap $] you said you need to find, that's [small fraction]. If it performs, and it usually does, most GCs keep it running as an ongoing channel."

## 08: Close (ask once, cleanly, no softening)
> "Based on everything we've discussed, does it make sense to move forward with this?"

**Then stop talking.**

**If they hesitate, answer with a question, never a rebuttal:**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "That's a lot of money." | "Compared to what? The [gap $] you said you need to find?" → "Is the concern that the gap isn't big enough, or that you're not sure this closes enough of it?" |
| "Need to think about it." | "Totally fair. What part of what we covered is still unclear?" |
| "Need to talk to my partner." | "Makes sense. What would make it easiest to bring it to them? Want me on that call?" |
| "Not a good time." | "What would need to change for it to be a good time?" |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | "I'd rather change the scope than the price. Which part would you take out?" |

# PART 3: CALL 2, THE CLOSE (only if needed)

15 to 20 minutes. Their partner or CFO is on the call if needed.

**1. Reanchor (read it from your Numbers Sheet):**
> "Before we go through the plan, let me play back what you told me. The goal is [goal]. Today you're at [today]. That's about [N] more jobs, around [gap $] in gross profit. And when I asked what the next 6 to 12 months look like if nothing changes, you said '[their quote].' Is that still accurate?"

> "That's the case you built. I just wrote it down."

**2. Walk through the plan and get their input on each section:**
> "Let's go through the plan. Stop me on anything that doesn't fit."

After each section: *"Does that fit, or would you change anything?"* Say yes to every reasonable change.

**Spend the most time on the opportunity criteria.** They need to approve exactly what counts:
> "This is the important part. An owner only counts if they're in your service area, have a [project type] project of at least [$X], starting within [12] months, and agree to a walkthrough or ask you for pricing. Does that match what you'd call a real opportunity?"

**3. Watch for "what happens next?" or "when would we start?"** That's your signal. Go straight to the ask.

**4. Pricing (07) if you haven't covered it yet, then the Close (08):**
> "Based on everything we've covered, I'd like to send you the agreement today. Does that make sense to move forward?"

**5. They say yes:** agreement and invoice within the hour (50% now, 50% at day 45). Book the kickoff date on the call.

---

## Quick reference

**Intro call:** Opening → Connecting (why they replied) → Situation (how they win work, % negotiated, backlog) → Problem awareness (what they don't like, how long, the cause, the impact, why outside help) → 20/80 overview → book Discovery.

**Discovery call:** 00 Frame → 01 Discovery + goal → 02 Pain + the gap number → 03 Failed attempts → 04 Boxing (internal solve, belief, positive future, negative future, timing) + who decides → 05 Transition → 06 Pitch → **decision point** → 07 Pricing → 08 Close, OR book Call 2.

**Call 2:** Reanchor → walk through the plan (approve the criteria) → Pricing → Close.
