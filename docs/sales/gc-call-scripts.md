# Commercial GC: Call Scripts

Adapted from the GapSi method (termsheetinator/gapsi-agent) for the Commercial Bid Pipeline offer.
Buyer type: **Old Economy.** Talk about relationships, steady work and protecting what they've built.
Never say "AI", "outbound", "automation" or "sequences" on a call. The mechanism stays under the hood.

Rule zero: **no price until the prospect has said the gap number out loud.**

---

## 1. Cold call opener (use sparingly, after an email touch)

> "Hey [Name], it's Charles. I sent you a note last week about getting in front of property owners before their projects go out to bid. Did I catch you at a bad time?"

If they're open:

> "Quick question. Right now, how much of your work is negotiated versus stuff you're bidding against five other GCs?"

Let them answer. Then:

> "Most owners I talk to want more of the negotiated side and less bid-board work. I help with that specifically. Worth 20 minutes next week to see if it fits? I've got Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 2."

If they say "send me info":

> "Happy to. What would you need to see to know if a 20 minute call is worth it?"

---

## 2. Discovery call (call 1 of 2, about 30 min)

### Opening: set the frame
> "Before I tell you anything about what I do, I want to understand how you win work today. So the first part is me asking questions. That work?"

### Phase 1: Reference point (their goal, their number)
> "Where do you want revenue or backlog to be by the end of next year?"
> "When you say more work, what does that look like in a number? Jobs, dollars, months of backlog?"
> "So the target is [X]. Is that right?"

*They must say the number. You never say it for them.*

### Phase 2: Current reality
> "Where are you today against that?"
> "How many months of backlog do you have right now?"
> "Of the jobs you won last year, how many were negotiated versus hard bid?"
> "What's your win rate on hard bids versus negotiated?"
> "What's a typical job worth to you in gross profit?"
> "What have you just accepted as normal that probably shouldn't be?"

### Phase 3: Gap calculation (they do the math)
> "So the target is [X], you're at [Y]. That's a [Z] gap. Does that track?"
> "If one negotiated job is worth about [$GP] to you, and you need [N] more of them, that's [N x $GP] sitting in the gap. Is that fair?"
> "What have you tried to close it? Why hasn't that worked?"
> "Who else feels it if that gap stays open? Your PMs, your crews, your bank?"

### Phase 4: How work comes in (the "what happens next" chain)
> "Walk me through how a new job finds you, from first contact to signed contract."
> "What happens next? And then? Who's involved at that stage?"
> "Who does business development today? How many hours a week does that actually get?"
> "So what I'm hearing is: [their process]. Is that right?"

### Phase 5: Inaction preview (light touch)
> "What happens if nothing changes between now and next spring?"

### Phase 6: Decision process
> "When I put together a plan for you to review, does that decision sit with you, or does a partner or your CFO look at it too?"

### Prescriptive close (never end open-ended)
> "Based on what you said, there's a real gap between [X] and [Y]. I'll come back with a plan built around your market and the owners I'd go after first. Does [day] at [time] work to go through it?"
> "One ask before then: when I send it, give me your first reaction. What fits, what's off, what's missing."

---

## 3. Close call (call 2 of 2)

### Reanchor
> "Last time you said the goal was [X], you're at [Y], and each negotiated job is worth about [$GP]. Still accurate?"

### Inaction cost (rotate frames until one lands)
- **Risk:** "What's riskier: trying something new, or going into next year with [Y] months of backlog?"
- **Goal:** "Current pace gets you [Y]. The target is [X]. What has to change?"
- **Cost:** "What does a slow quarter cost you? Idle crews, low bids just to keep people busy?"
- **Timing:** "If this doesn't change now, when does it?"

*Ask, then stop talking.*

### Reframe
> "So the question isn't whether this costs something. It's whether the gap is more expensive than fixing it."

### Present the offer (loss prevention, not spend)
> "Here's what I'd do. Over 90 days I build a system that gets you in front of property owners, developers and facility managers in [City] before their projects go out to bid. You take the meetings, I handle everything else, and you own it at the end. The target is 8 or more of those meetings in the first 45 days. If it falls short, I keep working until it gets there."
> "The investment is [$25k]. Measured against the [N x $GP] gap you walked me through, does that make sense?"

### The ask
> "Based on everything you've told me, does it make sense to get started?"

If yes, set the next step on the call: agreement, invoice, and a kickoff date.

---

## 4. Objection handlers (never rebut, answer with a question)

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "That's a lot of money." | "Compared to what? The [N x $GP] gap you mentioned?" Then: "Is the concern that the gap isn't big enough, or that you're not sure this closes enough of it?" |
| "Need to think about it." | "Totally fair. What part of what we covered is still unclear?" |
| "We tried marketing before and it didn't work." | "What happened? What did you expect versus what you got?" |
| "We get our work from relationships." | "Makes sense. How many new relationships did you start last year, and who started them?" |
| "Now's not a good time." | "What would need to change for it to be a good time?" |
| "Send me something." | "Happy to. What specifically would help you decide if it's worth continuing?" |
| "We're talking to someone else." | "What are they solving that this doesn't?" |
| Silence after the call | Don't chase. Send one useful thing (for example, 5 property owners in their area planning work) with no ask. Then one short "worth a look this week?" |

---

## The one sentence to remember
**The fee is never the question. The gap is.**
