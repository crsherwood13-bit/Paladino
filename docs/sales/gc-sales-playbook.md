# Commercial GC Sales Playbook (follow it exactly)

You don't need to improvise. Every stage below has the exact words, what to write down, and what to do if they say something unexpected. If a situation is not covered, use the **one fallback line**:

> "Good question. Let me make sure I give you a real answer. Can I come back to you on that by tomorrow?"

Then write it down and figure it out after the call.

The whole process, start to finish:

```
Reply comes in → Book call 1 → Call 1 (discovery, 30 min) → Send 1-page scope (within 24h)
→ Call 2 (scope review + close, 20 min) → Agreement + invoice same day → Kickoff
```

Three rules that never bend:
1. **Never say the price before they've said their gap number out loud.**
2. **Never argue with an objection. Answer it with a question from the cheat sheet.**
3. **Never say "AI", "outbound", "automation", "sequences", "leads" or "funnel" to a GC.** Say "meetings with property owners", "negotiated work", "steady backlog".

---

## STAGE 1: When a reply comes in

Answer within 2 hours during the workday. Copy the matching reply.

**They say "interested" / "tell me more" / "sure":**
> Great. Easiest is a quick 20 minute call so I can see if this fits how you win work. Does Tuesday at 10am or Thursday at 2pm work better? Happy to do early morning or after 4 if you're on a jobsite.

**They ask "how much?":**
> Depends on your market and how many owners we're going after. Usually it's a fraction of what one negotiated job is worth to you. Easiest to figure out on a 20 minute call. Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 2?

(Do NOT give a number by email. Ever.)

**They ask "send me info first":**
> Happy to. What would you need to see to know if a 20 minute call is worth your time?

Whatever they answer, send 3 to 5 sentences answering exactly that, then: "Want to grab 20 minutes Tuesday or Thursday?"

**They ask "who are you / how does it work?":**
> I build a system that gets commercial GCs in front of property owners, developers and facility managers before their projects go out to bid. You take the meetings, I handle everything else. Worth 20 minutes to see if it'd work in [City]? Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 2?

**They say "we get all our work from relationships":**
> Makes sense, most good GCs do. This is how you start more of those relationships without you doing the legwork. Worth 20 minutes?

**They say "not interested" / "remove me":**
> Understood, I'll take you off. Appreciate the reply.

Then remove them. No second try.

## STAGE 2: Booking the call

As soon as they pick a time, send this:

> Locked in for [Day] at [Time] [Timezone]. I'll call you at [their phone number]. Is that the best number? Before we talk, it'd help to have a rough sense of your backlog and what a typical job is worth to you. No prep needed beyond that.

Calendar invite title: **"[Their Company] + Charles: winning more negotiated work"**

Day before at 7am, text or email:
> Hi [Name], still good for [Time] tomorrow?

If no answer by noon the day of, call them once at the time anyway.

**No-show:** wait 10 minutes, then send:
> Looks like today got away from you. Happens on jobsites. Want to try [new day] at [time] or [other day] at [time]?

Two no-shows means move on.

## STAGE 3: Before call 1 (15 minutes of prep)

Fill this in from their website, LinkedIn and Google. Have it open during the call.

```
Company:                       City:
Owner name:                    Years in business:
What they build: (TI / ground-up / medical / retail / industrial / etc.)
2 recent projects from their website:
Approx staff (LinkedIn):
5 property owners / developers / facility managers in their city I'd go after:
  1.
  2.
  3.
  4.
  5.
```

The 5 names are your secret weapon. You'll use them at the end of call 1 and they make you look like you already did the work.

## STAGE 4: Call 1, Discovery (30 minutes)

Have the **Numbers Sheet** below open. Your only job on this call is to **fill every blank in the Numbers Sheet.** You are not selling. You are asking.

```
NUMBERS SHEET
Goal (revenue or backlog, by when):          ______
Today (revenue or backlog right now):        ______
Months of backlog right now:                 ______
% of work negotiated vs hard bid:            ______ / ______
Win rate on hard bids:                       ______
Win rate on negotiated work:                 ______
Gross profit on a typical job ($):           ______
Jobs needed to hit goal:                     ______
GAP ($) = jobs needed x GP per job:          ______  ← they must say this out loud
Who does business development today:         ______
Hours per week on it:                        ______
Who else signs off on a decision:            ______
```

### Minute 0 to 2: Open
Say exactly:
> "Thanks for making time. Before I tell you anything about what I do, I want to understand how you win work today. So the first part is me asking questions. That work?"

Wait for "yes".

### Minute 2 to 7: Their goal
Ask these in order. Write each answer on the sheet.
1. > "Where do you want the business to be by the end of next year? Revenue, backlog, whatever number you watch."
2. If vague ("more work", "keep growing"):
   > "If next year went great, what's the number?"
3. Repeat it back:
   > "So the target is [their number]. Is that right?"

Wait for "yes". If they correct you, write the corrected number.

### Minute 7 to 15: Where they are today
Ask these in order:
1. > "Where are you today against that?"
2. > "How many months of backlog do you have right now?"
3. > "Of the jobs you won last year, roughly what share was negotiated versus hard bid?"
4. > "When you hard bid, how often do you win? And negotiated?"
5. > "On a typical job, what do you clear in gross profit? Ballpark is fine."
6. > "Who handles bringing in new work today? How many hours a week does that really get?"

**If they won't give a number:** say
> "No problem, just a range. Is it closer to $50k or $200k?"

Ranges are fine. Write the middle of the range.

### Minute 15 to 20: The gap (THE most important part)
Do the math on your sheet first: (Goal − Today) ÷ GP per job ≈ jobs needed.

Then say:
> "So you want to be at [goal], you're at [today]. Roughly how many more jobs is that?"

Let THEM answer. If they say a number, write it. If they hesitate, say:
> "If a typical job clears about [GP], it'd be around [your math] more jobs. Does that sound right?"

Then:
> "So that's about [jobs x GP] in gross profit you need to find that isn't on the books yet. Is that fair?"

**Stop talking. Wait for them to say yes.** This is the number the whole sale is built on. Circle it.

Then ask one of these (pick one, don't read all):
> "What have you tried to close that gap?"
> "Why hasn't that worked?"

### Minute 20 to 24: How work finds them
> "Walk me through how a new job finds you, from first contact to signed contract."

Every time they pause, say only:
> "And what happens next?"

When they finish, repeat it back:
> "So what I'm hearing is [their process in 1 sentence]. Is that right?"

### Minute 24 to 26: Doing nothing
Ask once:
> "What happens if nothing changes between now and next spring?"

Write their answer word for word. You'll quote it on call 2.

### Minute 26 to 28: Who decides
> "When I put a plan together for you, does that decision sit with you, or does a partner or your CFO look at it too?"

If someone else: "Would it make sense to have them on the next call?"

### Minute 28 to 30: Close the call (always the same)
> "Based on what you told me, there's a real gap between [goal] and [today], about [gap $]. I'll put together a one page plan built around [City] and the owners I'd go after first. Actually, a few I'd start with: [read 2 or 3 of your 5 names]."

(Pause. Let them react. If they say "oh I know them" or "we've wanted to get in there", write it down.)

> "I'll send the plan by tomorrow. Can we do 20 minutes on [day, 2 to 4 days out] at [time] to go through it?"

> "One ask: when I send it, give me your first reaction. What fits, what's off, what's missing. That's what makes the next call useful."

Book it before you hang up.

### Disqualify (politely end, don't send a scope) if:
- They mostly do residential.
- Revenue under about $3M, or they can't take on more work (crews maxed out).
- They want "leads" to call, not meetings.
- They would not say any numbers at all, even ranges.

Say:
> "Honestly, I don't think I'm the right fit right now, and I'd rather tell you that than waste your money. If [the reason] changes, reach out."

## STAGE 5: Send the 1-page scope (within 24 hours)

Copy this, fill the brackets, send as a PDF or in the email body.

```
[Company] + Charles: Commercial Pipeline Plan

WHERE YOU ARE
You said the goal is [goal]. Today you're at [today], with [X] months of backlog.
About [Y]% of your work is hard bid, where you win roughly [Z]%.
To hit the goal you need about [N] more jobs, roughly [gap $] in gross profit.
In your words: "[quote from the 'nothing changes' question]"

WHAT I'LL DO
1. Build your list of property owners, developers and facility managers in [City],
   starting with [3 of your names].
2. Set up the system to reach them from your company, in your name.
3. Run it. Interested owners get booked straight onto your calendar.
4. Hand it over at the end. You own all of it.

WHAT YOU DO
Approve the list and the message (about 1 hour total). Take the meetings.

TIMELINE
Week 1 to 2: build. Week 3: first outreach. First meetings typically weeks 3 to 5.
Target: [8] meetings with owners who have upcoming projects within [45] days.
If we come up short, I keep working until we hit it, no extra charge.

INVESTMENT
[PRICE], one time. Measured against the [gap $] gap above.

Questions or changes? Reply with your first reaction before [call 2 date].
```

Email to send it with:
> Hi [Name], here's the plan we talked about. Take a look and send me your first reaction before [day]: what fits, what's off, what's missing. Talk [day] at [time].

## STAGE 6: Call 2, Scope review + close (20 minutes)

### Minute 0 to 3: Recap their case (read it, it's from your sheet)
> "Before we go through the plan, let me play back what you told me. The goal is [goal]. Today you're at [today]. That's about [N] more jobs, around [gap $] in gross profit. And when I asked what happens if nothing changes, you said '[their quote]'. Is that still accurate?"

Wait for "yes".

> "That's the case you built. I just wrote it down."

### Minute 3 to 12: Go through the plan, get their fingerprints
> "Let's walk through the plan. Stop me on anything that doesn't fit."

Go section by section. After each section ask:
> "Does that fit, or would you change anything?"

Write down every change they ask for. **Say yes to every reasonable change** (different owner types, a different city, timing). Each change makes it more theirs.

### Minute 12 to 15: Watch for the signal
If they start asking **"so what happens next?"** or **"when would this start?"** → go straight to the ask below.

If they're still making changes → that's fine. Say:
> "Let me update the plan with those changes and send it back tonight."

Then go to the ask anyway.

### Minute 15 to 18: The ask (say exactly this)
> "Based on everything we've covered, I'd like to send you the agreement today. Does that make sense to move forward?"

**Stop talking. Do not say anything until they answer.** Silence is fine. Count to 10 in your head if you need to.

- **"Yes"** → go to Stage 7.
- **Anything else** → use the cheat sheet below. Then ask again:
  > "So does it make sense to move forward?"

Ask a maximum of 2 times on the call. If still no, book a final 10 minute call within 3 days.

---

## OBJECTION CHEAT SHEET (say only the line in the right column)

| They say | You say (then stop talking) |
|---|---|
| "That's a lot of money." | "Compared to what? The [gap $] you said you need to find?" |
| (if they push again on price) | "Is the concern that the gap isn't big enough, or that you're not sure this closes enough of it?" |
| "I need to think about it." | "Totally fair. What part of what we covered is still unclear?" |
| "I need to talk to my partner / CFO." | "Makes sense. What would make it easiest to bring to them? Want me on that call?" |
| "Now's not a good time." | "What would need to change for it to be a good time?" |
| "We tried marketing before, didn't work." | "What happened? What did you expect versus what you got?" |
| "We get work from relationships." | "How many new relationships did you start last year, and who started them?" |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | "I'd rather change the scope than the price. Which part of the plan would you take out?" |
| "Can I pay after results?" | "The guarantee covers that. If we don't hit [8] meetings, I keep working until we do." |
| "We're talking to someone else." | "What are they solving that this doesn't?" |
| "Send me the agreement and I'll look." | "Will do. What would stop you from signing it this week?" |

**Never** lower the price on the call. **Never** say "I understand, but…". Ask the question, then stop.

---

## STAGE 7: They said yes

On the call:
> "Great. I'll send the agreement and invoice in the next hour. Once that's done, kickoff is [date]. You'll need about 30 minutes that week to approve the list."

Within 1 hour, send:
- The agreement (scope from Stage 5 + payment terms).
- The invoice. Terms: **100% upfront** at $7.5k. **50% to start, 50% at day 30** at $15k and up.

When paid, send:
> Payment received, thanks [Name]. Kickoff is [date]. I'll send the owner list for your approval by [date].

---

## STAGE 8: They went quiet

| When | What to send |
|---|---|
| Day 2 after call 2 | "Hi [Name], any questions on the plan or the agreement?" |
| Day 5 | One useful thing, no ask: "Saw [owner from your list] just bought / is planning [X]. Figured you'd want to know." |
| Day 10 | "Should I keep this open for [Month], or close it out for now?" |
| After that | Stop. Put a reminder for 60 days. |

---

## After every call (5 minutes)

Write down:
1. The gap number they said.
2. Their exact words on "what happens if nothing changes".
3. Every objection they raised, word for word.
4. What you said back and how they reacted.

After 5 calls, read these back. The same 2 or 3 objections will keep showing up. That's what to get better at.
