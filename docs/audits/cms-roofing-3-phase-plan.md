## Phase 1: Base. Make the data tell a story. (Weeks 1–4)

### What I pull
- CRM / job software: every lead, estimate, and job (won, lost, open)
- Marketing spend by channel: ads, HomeAdvisor/Angi, SEO, print, sponsorships, canvassing
- Call logs / call tracking, and the website form inbox
- Invoices / job revenue and job costs (for margin)
- Past-customer list, and the commercial accounts

### The KPIs, defined once so everyone uses the same numbers

| KPI | Formula | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **CPL**: cost per lead | Channel spend ÷ leads from that channel | What does it cost to make the phone ring? |
| **CPA**: cost per appointment | Channel spend ÷ inspections held from that channel | What does it cost to get in front of a buyer? |
| **CAC**: cost per acquired customer | (Channel spend + sales cost) ÷ signed jobs from that channel | What does a new customer really cost? |
| **Lead → inspection %** | Inspections ÷ leads | Is the office converting what comes in? |
| **Close rate** | Signed ÷ inspections (by source, by rep) | Are the reps converting? |
| **Average ticket** | Revenue ÷ jobs (repair / replace / insurance / commercial) | What's a job worth? |
| **Gross margin** | (Revenue − materials − labor − subs) ÷ revenue | What do we actually keep? |
| **LTV**: lifetime value | Margin on first job + repeat work (repairs, gutters, siding, maintenance) + margin from referrals | What's a customer worth over time? |
| **LTV : CAC** | LTV ÷ CAC | Is this channel worth more money? (target 3:1+) |
| **Revenue per lead** | Revenue from a channel ÷ its leads | The one number to compare every channel on |
| **Speed-to-lead** | Minutes from inquiry to first human contact | Are we losing leads before we talk to them? |
| **Unsold estimate $** | Dollar value of estimates given, not signed | How much money is sitting on the table? |
| **Backlog** | Signed $ not yet installed | Can crews handle more sales? |
| **Payback period** | CAC ÷ monthly margin per customer (maintenance plans) | How fast does recurring revenue pay back? |

### Turning it into a story
The numbers get turned into answers:
1. **Where the money comes from:** revenue, margin, and LTV by channel and by job type.
2. **Where it leaks:** a funnel waterfall (leads → contacted → inspected → quoted → signed → installed) showing exactly where people drop.
3. **What each channel is worth:** CPL, CPA, CAC, and revenue per lead side by side, to decide where to double down and what to cut.
4. **Seasonality & storms:** how much of the year depends on weather, and what that means for cash and staffing.
5. **The forecast baseline:** `leads × lead→inspection % × close % × average ticket`, by month. This is what Phase 2 and 3 improvements get measured against.

### Phase 1 deliverables
- **State of Revenue report**, 5–7 pages written in plain language, walked through in person
- **Live KPI dashboard** (CRM reporting or Looker Studio), updated weekly
- **Weekly one-page KPI summary** with 3 bullets: what moved, why, and what we're doing about it
- **A ranked list of the top 5 revenue leaks**, with a dollar estimate on each. This becomes the Phase 2 priority list.

**Phase 1 is done when:** every KPI above has a real number, and you agree with the story.

## Phase 2: Optimize what you already have. Add AI where it helps. (Weeks 3–8)

The priority order comes from the Phase 1 leak list. Expected work:

### Lead forms & capture
- Form rebuilt with address, service type, insurance-claim yes/no, and SMS consent
- Auto-reply on; instant text-back for forms and missed calls
- After-hours and weekend coverage
- Listing cleanup: one name/address/phone everywhere; claim every profile

### Nurture sequences (already written; loaded and tuned to CMS's numbers)
- **New lead:** text in seconds, call in 2 minutes, 5-day follow-up; offers two specific times, not a link
- **Lead went cold:** 30-day re-engage
- **Estimate not signed:** 90-day follow-up with financing and proof angles; the "which one is closest?" email
- **Lost / not now:** 6- and 12-month check-ins
- **Past customers:** yearly check-up, post-storm check, referral ask, gutters/siding, warranty reminders
- **Dead database:** a one-time "are you still looking?" message to every old lead

### Prospecting & lead data
- CRM cleaned: duplicates merged, every record tagged by source, job type, segment, and status
- Past customers and old estimates enriched (email, phone, property data) so they can be worked
- Commercial and property-manager lists built from county property records + contact data, ready for Phase 3

### AI, wherever it earns its keep
| Where | What the AI does | Human stays in the loop for |
|---|---|---|
| **After-hours calls** | AI receptionist answers, collects the details, books or flags urgent leaks | Every lead gets a human callback the next morning |
| **Call notes** | Calls transcribed and summarized straight into the CRM | Reps review the summary |
| **Reply drafting** | Drafts responses to leads and outbound replies from the approved reply playbook | A person reads and sends |
| **Lead scoring** | Scores leads by job type, property value, roof age, and storm exposure, so the best get called first | The office sets the rules |
| **Estimate follow-up** | Personalizes follow-ups using the inspection notes and photos | The rep approves |
| **Review replies** | Drafts a reply to every Google review | The owner approves negative-review replies |
| **Content** | Turns job photos into Google Business posts, social posts, and case studies | A final look before posting |
| **KPI narrative** | Writes the weekly "what moved and why" summary from the dashboard | Charles checks it |
| **Outbound research** | Pulls building details and roof age, and drafts Loom talking points | Every claim is verified before it's sent |

*Guardrail: AI voice is used for **answering** inbound calls only. Outbound AI voice calls need the person's prior consent under FCC rules, so we don't do them cold.*

**Phase 2 is done when:** speed-to-lead is under 5 minutes, every sequence is live, and the Phase 1 dashboard shows the lift. Tracked targets: lead → inspection %, unsold-estimate recovery $, and revenue per lead.

## Phase 3: Outbound + split testing against current inbound (Weeks 7–13, then ongoing)

### The test grid

**Offer (number) × Copy template (letter)**

| | **A. Clear pitch** | **B. Lead magnet** | **C. Loom** | **D. Local contractor** | **E. Soft trigger** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Full reroof** | 1A | 1B | 1C | 1D | 1E |
| **2. Maintenance plan** | 2A | 2B | 2C | 2D | 2E |

- **Offer 1: Full reroof.** A free condition report and written replacement plan/budget, leading to a reroof proposal.
- **Offer 2: Maintenance plan.** Two inspections a year, priority leak response, and a written report after each visit, leading to recurring revenue and first call on the eventual reroof.

- **A. Clear pitch:** say exactly what they get and ask.
- **B. Lead magnet:** give a useful 1-pager first.
- **C. Loom:** a 60–90 second personalized video of *their* building from aerial imagery.
- **D. Local contractor:** lead with being local since 2006, crews nearby, not a storm-chaser.
- **E. Soft trigger:** tie to something real about them: roof age, a recent sale, their budget season, a job nearby.

**Naming convention:** `[Segment]-[Offer][Copy]-[Channel]`
Example: `COM-2C-LI` = Commercial, Maintenance plan, Loom, LinkedIn.
Segments: `COM` commercial · `PM` property managers/investors · `HOA` associations.
Channels: `EM` email · `LI` LinkedIn.

### How we run it (small local market, so tests are staged)

| Round | Weeks | Test | Held constant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1: Offer** | 7–8 | **1A vs 2A**: reroof vs maintenance, same clear-pitch copy | Copy, segment (COM), channel |
| **2: Copy** | 9–10 | Winning offer × **B, C, D, E** (e.g. 2B / 2C / 2D / 2E) | Offer, segment, channel |
| **3: Channel** | 11–12 | Top 2 cells on **EM vs LI** | Offer + copy |
| **4: Segment** | 13+ | Winning cell on **PM and HOA** | Offer + copy + channel |

**Sample size:** about 150 contacts per cell, minimum. The Bowling Green/Owensboro commercial market is small, so cells stay lean and nobody gets hit twice by different tests.

**Scoring:** positive reply rate → meetings booked → condition reports → proposals $ → signed $.

**Decision rules:** under 1% positive replies after 150 contacts → kill. 1–3% → iterate. Over 3% → scale, and it becomes the control for the next round.

### Follow-ups (shared by every cell, same thread)
- **Email 2 (Day 4):** "Following up, {{first_name}}. Are roofs something you handle at {{company}}, or is there someone better to ask?"
- **Email 3 (Day 9):** One real result from a similar building, then "Worth a look at yours?"
- **Email 4 (Day 16, new thread):** A one-line question. "Who gets the call when the roof leaks at {{company}}?"
- **Email 5 (Day 21):** Breakup. "I'll stop here. If the roof becomes a priority, reply and we'll have a report to you within the week."

### LinkedIn versions (same offer and letter, rewritten for LinkedIn)
- **Connection request (all cells):** "Hi {{first_name}}, I work with facilities folks around Bowling Green and Owensboro. Good to be connected." *(No pitch.)*
- **Message 1, after they accept:** the email 1 copy for that cell, cut to 2–3 sentences, ending in a question
- **Message 2 (Day 5):** "Did that land, {{first_name}}? Happy to send the [guide / video / report details] if it's useful."
- **Loom cells (C) on LinkedIn:** send the video link in message 1 when the video is already recorded. It gets watched far more often there than in cold email.

### When someone says yes
Two specific times within minutes, a short qualifying call, contact on the second channel the same day, and a full handoff note to the closer. The pre-written answers to every reply type are in the Sequence System doc.
