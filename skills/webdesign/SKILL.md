# Web dev tips: #webdesign (conversion & landing pages)

Sourced by web-search indexing of public X posts on 2026-07-08 (see `skills/css/SKILL.md`
for sourcing method).

- **@fekdaoui**: high-converting landing pages clarify the message, write customer-focused
  copy, keep the design clean, limit the visible feature list to 3–5 points, and build trust
  through social proof. — [source](https://x.com/fekdaoui/status/1800878865314656302)
- **@ModestMitkus**: a 10-step high-converting landing-page formula from a 5-year UI/UX
  designer — hero clarity, single CTA, proof above the fold, friction removal in forms.
- **@lennysan**: cites an Unbounce benchmark report — conversion-rate expectations vary
  wildly by industry, so "5% vs 10%" isn't meaningful without an industry baseline.
- **@uxlinks**: landing-page cheatsheet emphasizing one primary action per screen.

## Applied to Paladino — with a caveat

Most of this genre (urgency, single big CTA, growth-hacky copy) is written for
consumer/SaaS landing pages and directly conflicts with `docs/ib-design-reference.md`,
which found the opposite pattern across 15 real investment-banking sites: restraint,
minimal CTA clutter, and credibility signals (scale, longevity, discretion) instead of
conversion pressure. For `site/index.html` we took only the parts that don't fight that:
one clear primary action (`Start a Conversation`), a single-purpose hero, and social proof
via the stats band / transactions grid — not urgency language, feature-count limits, or a
hard sales CTA, which would read as off-brand for a boutique advisory firm.
