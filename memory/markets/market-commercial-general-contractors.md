# Market — Commercial General Contractors
## The problem (plain)
They wait for work to find them (bid boards, referrals, the same few developers). When the backlog thins, the owner scrambles and bids low on open jobs everyone else is bidding.
## What it costs them (the gap)
One missed negotiated project = tens to hundreds of thousands in gross profit. Open public bids are crowded and low-margin; invited/negotiated work wins more often at better margins. Thin backlog = idle crews + forced low bids.
## Who you talk to
Commercial GCs (tenant improvement, ground-up, design-build), US, 11–50 staff.
## Who owns the problem
Owner / President / Principal; at the top of the band, VP/Director of Business Development or Preconstruction.
## Size segmentation
1–10 too small for budget · 11–50 owner feels it + can sign $5k (pick) · 51–100 BD lead exists, budget exists (secondary) · 100+ have BD teams, slower buy.
## Forcing function / why now
Backlog thinning; commercial slowdown; Q4 planning for next year's work.
## Awareness / sophistication
Problem-aware, solution-unaware. Low sophistication — almost never pitched outbound for owners/developers.
